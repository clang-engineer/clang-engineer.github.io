# Scene 5: No-seung Dance — 노승춤

## Characters

- **No-seung (노승)** — old Buddhist monk
- **Malttugi (말뚝이)**
- **Choegwari (최괄이)**
- **Saemaeksi (새맥시)**

## Costume and Props

No-seung wears a **songnak**, a **108-bead rosary (baekpal yeomju)**, a **moktak**, and a red **gasa**, and carries a **yukhwanjang (육환장)**, a ringed Buddhist staff.

## Performance Script

*No-seung has spent his life in a temple studying Buddhist teachings and preparing for the life to come. Wanting to see the world, he leaves the temple. After drinking chrysanthemum wine to intoxication, he descends from the mountains, wanders through the secular world, and, on his way toward Cheongnyongsa at Gwangdeoksan, staggers into the performance ground and collapses.*

*Entering to Jungjungmori rhythm, he sings a monk's song.*

**NO-SEUNG:** Deep in green waters and blue mountains, every flower is in bloom; phoenixes and peacocks call, butterflies dance in pairs, and the flowing water winds bend after bend, striking the screen-like rocks...

*Drunk, he staggers and collapses near the center. Suddenly the sound of* samhyun-yukgak *instrumental music rings out. He comes to his senses and dances to Yeongsan Dodeuri jangdan. Still prone, he raises his head and looks to both sides. Gradually recovering his strength, he dances while surveying the world around him, then sighs deeply.*

**NO-SEUNG:** When shall I ever reach Cheongnyongsa at Gwangdeoksan by the Western Sea?

*He sits, strikes the moktak, and recites passages from the* **Cheonsugyeong (천수경, Thousand Hands Sutra)**.

**NO-SEUNG:** Jeonggueop Jineon—*Surisuri mahasuri susuri sabaha.*  
Obang Naeoe Anwi Jesin Jineon—*Namusamanda motdanam, om dorodoro jimi sabaha.*  
*Musang simsim mimyobeop, baekcheonman-geop nanjou, ageum mungyeon deuksuji, wonhae yeorae jinsirui...*  
*Namu Amitabha, Gwanseeum Bosal.*

> The Buddhist mantras are retained primarily in transliteration. Their ritual sound and textual identity are more important here than replacing them with invented English liturgical equivalents.

*While No-seung is reciting, Malttugi and Choegwari enter and mock him.*

**MALTTUGI:** Swi—! Hey, hey! Look at that monk. He's no ordinary fellow. Let's bring our Saemaeksi out and have some fun with him.

**CHOEGWARI:** That's a fine idea.

*They leave, return with Saemaeksi, place her near the center, and begin singing* **Daekko Taryeong (대꼬타령)** *to Jajinmori jangdan.*

### Daekko Taryeong

**MALTTUGI & CHOEGWARI:**  
Gaegol, gaegol, blue gaegol—  
ehe, ehe, eheya!  
If you seek Gaegol's house,  
come to the water-parsley field.

Ehe, eheya!  
The maidens of Eunyul and Jangyeon  
go out to pick cotton.  
Ehe-e-e, eheya!  
Pick it low and pick it high...

Ehe, ehe, eheya!  
The maidens of Hamgyeong  
go out selling wooden bowls...

Ehe, ehe, eheya!  
The maidens of Jeonggokgol  
go out selling fresh greens—  
bracken, fern shoots, wild greens,  
and mountain herbs from Guwolsan.

Ehe, ehe, eheya!  
The maidens of Woljeonggol  
go out selling pine nuts—  
one-eyed, empty-shelled, and double-eyed nuts are just the thing!

*As they sing, Saemaeksi dances coquettishly to entice No-seung. At first he repeatedly avoids her. Eventually he becomes captivated, stares at her, rises near the end of the song, and approaches as if to embrace her.*

**MALTTUGI:** Era, swi! Hey! Look at that monk's behavior—what a restless fellow. Let's tease him once more with **Byeongsin Nanbongga**.

**CHOEGWARI:** That's a fine idea.

*They begin* **Byeongsin Nanbongga (병신난봉가)** *to Gutgeori jangdan.*

### Byeongsin Nanbongga

**MALTTUGI & CHOEGWARI:**  
Ehe, ehe, eogeoya, eoya eoya, dieora—  
you are my love.

Look at that monk!  
Seven-bolt monk's robe, songnak on his head,  
a rosary of one hundred and eight beads around his neck,  
a red gasa over his shoulder—

E-e-e, eogeoya, eoya eoya, dieora—  
you are my love.

Ringed staff in his hand,  
drunk on chrysanthemum wine,  
swaying and staggering as he comes down—

E-e-e-e, eogeoya, eoya eoya, dieora—  
you are my love.

I'll give you all one hundred and eight beads;  
come, you and I, let us live together.

E-e-e, eogeoya, eoya eoya, dieora—  
you are my love.

He's gone wild, gone wild—  
the monk from the back temple has gone wild!

E-e-e-e, eogeoya, eoya eoya, dieora—  
you are my love.

There's no resisting it, no resisting it—  
even a monk is helpless before Saemaeksi!

*During the song, No-seung circles Saemaeksi in dance. Twice he tries to embrace her and is rejected. He finally places his rosary around her neck, and they dance together. Near the end, enraged by the song mocking him, No-seung strikes Malttugi across the face with his jangsam.*

**MALTTUGI:** Aigo! The monk is hitting people!

*He runs around the ground and exits. Choegwari, furious, steps into the center.*

**CHOEGWARI:** Era, swi—! That monk from the back temple is tough indeed! He ate bracken and fern shoots, bellflower root and deodeok, and drank the water from rotting wild ginseng on Guwolsan—no wonder he's tough! Hey, monk! This time let's go to Jeongbang Fortress in Hwangju!

**BULLIM:** *“Under the clear blue sky and bright sun, purple clouds arise.”*

*No-seung and Choegwari engage in a prolonged dance confrontation. No-seung strikes Choegwari in the face and sends him backward.*

**CHOEGWARI:** Swi—! This time I'll drink some cold water and try again. A goblin's club is just the thing for that monk. Let's beat him with a goblin's club!

**BULLIM:** *“Oak firewood, blazing firewood!”*

*To Jajeun Domburi jangdan, Choegwari dances while mercilessly striking No-seung with his hansam and drives him out. No-seung staggers away.*

*Saemaeksi removes the rosary and stands near the front center. Choegwari approaches her from one side and then the other; each time she turns away.*

**CHOEGWARI:** Hey, woman! After roaming all ninety-nine valleys of Guwolsan with that back-temple monk, your body reeks of him. Achoo! Achoo! But never mind that—come and have some fun with me.

**BULLIM:** *“Blue pine and green bamboo—the integrity of the gentleman!”*

*They dance. Choegwari courts her from right and left, but she gives him no encouragement.*

**CHOEGWARI:** Swi—! You're a costly one, aren't you? Stubborn too. Let me try once more.

**BULLIM:** *“Among the flowers the butterflies dance, like swirling snow.”*

*Saemaeksi now dances with every manner of coquettish gesture. Choegwari laughs in satisfaction.*

**CHOEGWARI:** Swi—! That's more like it. You can't resist forever—not when it's Choegwari! Well then, you're my wife now. Let's have ourselves a proper dance.

**BULLIM:** *“Kwaengchang, kwaengchang, kwae-gwaengchang—!”*

*The two perform a lively dance together, and Choegwari leads Saemaeksi out.*

## Cultural and Translation Notes

The scene juxtaposes Buddhist ritual language and objects with worldly desire and comic ridicule. The translation therefore preserves the Buddhist vocabulary rather than converting it into generic “religious” language.

The *Cheonsugyeong* passage is ritual recitation. Transliteration is retained for the mantric portions; explanatory meanings belong in the guide or notes rather than being substituted into the spoken performance.

The songs *Daekko Taryeong* and *Byeongsin Nanbongga* use refrains and regional folk-song structures. Their vocables are preserved because they are musical performance language, not disposable filler.
