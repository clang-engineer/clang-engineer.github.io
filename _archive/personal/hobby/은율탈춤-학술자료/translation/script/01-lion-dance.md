# Scene 1: Saja-chum (사자춤) — Lion Dance

## Characters

- **Saja (사자)** — a large white lion operated by three performers

## Music

- **Taryeong jangdan (타령장단)**
- **Jajeun Domburi jangdan (잦은돔부리장단)**

## Performance Script

*After the* gilnori *ends, a “large white lion,” operated by three performers inside the costume, enters the performance ground. Accompanied by* Taryeong jangdan *and* Jajeun Domburi jangdan, *the lion performs a vigorous round of dancing and then exits.*

## Cultural and Performance Context

The position of the Lion Dance as the first scene is significant. The source explicitly describes it as having the character of a **byeoksa (벽사)** ritual dance: a dance intended to drive away harmful influences and ritually purify the performance ground.

The Eunyul Talchum lion is also distinctive in construction. Whereas the lion figures in some other Korean mask-dance and lion-play traditions are operated by two performers, the Eunyul lion contains **three performers**.

For the broader meaning of *byeoksa*, see the project guide.
