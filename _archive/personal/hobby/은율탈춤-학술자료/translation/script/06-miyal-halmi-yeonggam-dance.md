# Scene 6: Miyal Halmi and Yeonggam Dance — 미얄할미 영감춤

## Characters

- **Yeonggam (영감)** — elderly husband
- **Halmi / Miyal Halmi (할미 / 미얄할미)** — his elderly wife
- **Ttungttanjijip (뚱딴지집)** — Yeonggam's younger concubine
- **Malttugi**
- **Choegwari**
- **Mudang (무당)** — Korean shaman

## Opening

*Yeonggam enters in white trousers and jacket with a white* durumagi, *a* manggeon *and* gat *on his head, a mountain-shaped bundle on his back, a short tobacco pipe in his left hand, and a fan resting on his right shoulder. He dances into the center to Neut Taryeong jangdan.*

**YEONGGAM:** Era, swii—! I lived in Jeju, not far from the rock at Hallasan from which Go, Bu, and Yang—the Three Surnames—emerged. But life confined to an island became stifling, so I set out to see the Eight Provinces.

*He recounts a journey through the mountains and rivers of the eight provinces, finally reaching Bonguisan in Eunyul and arriving at the performance ground.*

**YEONGGAM:** The music is ringing out here! In my younger days I too loved music and merriment. I should dance a round before I go. The leaves may have died—but surely the stalk is not dead as well!

**BULLIM:** *“White-haired and idle among the cold mountains, yet the heart does not grow old.”*

*Yeonggam begins dancing to Domburi jangdan. During the second rhythmic cycle Halmi enters, dancing with her hips. She wears a white jacket and black skirt, carries a white cloth, and has a pair of straw sandals and a lunch bundle tied at her waist. She has wandered throughout the Eight Provinces searching for her husband and suddenly recognizes him.*

**HALMI:** *Grabbing him.* Yeonggam! Aren't you my Yeonggam?

**YEONGGAM:** Aigo, Halmi!

**HALMI:** Ah—I searched every corner of the Eight Provinces for you: every valley, every crack between the rocks, every grain of sand, every space between fallen leaves. And today I've finally found you again! Yeonggam—have you still been carrying that little thing around all this time? Ah, I'm glad to see you. So glad, Yeonggam!

## Naniga Taryeong

**HALMI:**  
So glad—so glad—  
to see my Yeonggam again,  
aigo, how truly glad I am—  
naniga nano, naniga nano...

Where—where have you been?  
Where have you gone,  
leaving me behind?  
Aigo, where have you been?

Like seeing rain at last  
after seven years of great drought—  
aigo, how truly glad I am!

*Near the final refrain, Ttungttanjijip, Yeonggam's younger concubine, enters. Seeing the reunion, she becomes angry and strikes Yeonggam on the shoulder. He whispers affectionately into her ear.*

**YEONGGAM:** Dear wife, my first wife has found me.

**HALMI:** *Seeing him with Ttungttanjijip.* What woman is this, trying to take my Yeonggam away?

**TTUNGTTANJIJIP:** Yeonggam! Who is this beggar-looking woman carrying on like this? Come, let's go.

**HALMI:** Hey, you! He's my Yeonggam. What sort of ridiculous woman are you, acting like this?

**TTUNGTTANJIJIP:** Who are you talking to? One look at you and anyone can see you're a beggar who used to follow firewood sellers around for scraps of food. Why do you keep calling our Yeonggam yours?

**HALMI:** He's my Yeonggam, not yours!

**TTUNGTTANJIJIP:** He's our Yeonggam, not yours!

*As the women quarrel, Malttugi and Choegwari enter.*

**CHOEGWARI:** Swii—! What's this? An old man here fighting between an old woman and a young one?

**HALMI:** I lived in Jeju near Hallasan, not far from the rock where Go, Bu, and Yang—the Three Surnames—emerged. My Yeonggam left three years ago to see the Eight Provinces. Today I finally found him again, but this absurd woman came along claiming that my Yeonggam is hers!

**TTUNGTTANJIJIP:** No! He's our Yeonggam, and she's the one claiming he's hers!

**MALTTUGI:** All right, all right. We'll settle this. Both of you, come here.

*Malttugi and Choegwari sit apart near the front. Both women approach, but Ttungttanjijip pushes Halmi aside and sits first.*

**CHOEGWARI:** Tell us something distinctive about Yeonggam.

**TTUNGTTANJIJIP:** Our Yeonggam eats well, takes things in stride, and is kind. That's what's distinctive about him.

**MALTTUGI:** That's it? Nothing else?

**TTUNGTTANJIJIP:** No, nothing else.

**CHOEGWARI:** Halmi, come here. Tell us something distinctive about him.

**HALMI:** Our Yeonggam likes scorched rice, and there's something hanging on him that's different from other men's.

**CHOEGWARI:** Something hanging on him? What is it?

**MALTTUGI:** Come on, Halmi. Tell us.

**HALMI:** Ah—our Yeonggam... *hesitating* th-that... that thing. Below his navel there's a mole the size of a bean, and right in the middle of it grows exactly one white hair.

**CHOEGWARI & MALTTUGI:** *Rushing toward Yeonggam.* Yeonggam, let's have a look!

**YEONGGAM:** *Angrily.* What are you doing? Are you officials or something? What gives you the right to look at another man's private parts?

**HALMI:** *Lifting his durumagi and miming pulling down his trousers.* Here, here—look!

*Malttugi and Choegwari nod to one another.*

**CHOEGWARI & MALTTUGI:** Halmi—he is certainly your Yeonggam.

**HALMI:** *Delighted, pushing the younger woman aside and taking Yeonggam's arm.* Yeonggam, let's go. Let's go!

**TTUNGTTANJIJIP:** *Shouting in resentment.* Yeonggam!

*She attacks Halmi. The two women grapple. Halmi is kicked by Ttungttanjijip and dies.*

## Halmi's Death and the Gut

*Yeonggam panics, checks Halmi's breath and pulse, realizes that she is dead, and wails.*

**YEONGGAM:** Aigo, Halmi! Aigo, Halmi!

*To Ttungttanjijip.*

Dear wife, my first wife is dead. Even death leaves no room for enmity. Poor Halmi—bring a *mudang* and have a *gut* performed so that she may go to a good place in the Pure Land.

*Ttungttanjijip exits and returns carrying a ritual table. Malttugi and Choegwari carry Halmi's body out. Mudang enters behind Ttungttanjijip, performs bows to the four directions, and faces front.*

## Mudang's Song

**MUDANG:** *Singing.*

**Awang Imgeum-ui Manseya**—  
*Refrain: Awang Imgeum-ui Manseya.*

May you live a thousand years, ten thousand years, a hundred million years—  
*Refrain.*

This saha world is Namsin-jubu—  
*Refrain.*

In the eastern land lies the nation of Joseon—  
*Refrain.*

The appointed ground is the sovereign's appointed ground—  
*Refrain.*

Within the sacred precinct is the domain of **Bugun-nim**—  
*Refrain.*

*The Mudang makes a broad circuit of the performance ground, then faces front.*

For the affairs of the nation, the Changes and the Eight Trigrams;  
for the spirit of the deceased, we send her to a good place—

**REFRAIN:** Aa, ee, ehe, ehee—send her to a good place.

Let the messengers who guide the dead—the Iljik Saja, Woljik Saja, and the Three Messengers—play their part, and let us send her to the Pure Land.

**REFRAIN:** Aa, ee, ehe, ehee—let us send her to the Pure Land.

As we send her to the lotus terrace of the blessed realm, may she escape the Hell of Knife Mountains, the Hell of Fire Mountains, the Hell of Venomous Snakes, the great-mountain hell, the blood hell, water hell, and ice hell—

**REFRAIN:** Aa, ee, ehe, ehee—may she escape the ice hell.

Where mountains are high, carry her over them; where waters are deep, ferry her across. Give abundant offerings of silver and money, and on the road she travels, escort her without suffering—

**REFRAIN:** Aa, ee, ehe, ehee—escort her without suffering.

*The Mudang dances and enters a state of spirit possession. She demonstrates the presence and power of the spirit through ritual actions with bells, fruit, knives, shadows, and dance. She later takes up a bolt of white cotton cloth and delivers a* **gongsu (공수)**—a spirit message—to Yeonggam.*

**MUDANG / SPIRIT OF HALMI:** Eoheonya—Yeonggam, I have come. I finally met the Yeonggam I longed for and missed so much, but because of Ttungttanjijip I could not live; I died before my time.

*Toward Ttungttanjijip.*

Heuk-heuk—Aigo, how bitter! Yeonggam, I will go on ahead. When we meet again in the other world, we shall talk of the steep ridges of great mountains, of the old tale of *Sukhyangjeon*, and of the stories from the *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*. Heuk-heuk—Yeonggam, this white cloth opens the road for me, and I shall go to a good place.

*The Mudang dances with the white cloth, strikes Ttungttanjijip with it, brings her before the ritual table, and makes her bow. She then blesses her for a good life. Malttugi and Choegwari stretch the white cloth into a long pathway. The Mudang performs ritual actions around and over it with incense, fan, and bells.*

**MUDANG:** Now—if you prefer Ttungttanjijip, fly to her; if you prefer Yeonggam, fly, fly to Yeonggam.

*With the rhythm, the cloth trembles and the fan and bells move toward Yeonggam.*

## Opening the Road to the Afterlife

*The Mudang begins to split the white cloth lengthwise, advancing a little farther with each refrain.*

**MUDANG:** Namu Amitabul.

**REFRAIN:** Namu Amitabul.

By command of the Ten Kings, your documents are held by the judge—

**REFRAIN:** Namu Amitabul.

When every charge and every deed is questioned—

**REFRAIN:** Namu Amitabul.

Before King Yeomna, solemnly, when you receive the command of the Ten Kings:  
Did you feed the hungry and earn the merit of giving food?  
Did you clothe those without clothes and accumulate virtue by saving lives?

**REFRAIN:** Namu Amitabul.

Were you filial to your parents?  
Did you refrain from treason and conspiracy?  
If you were loyal to the nation, go before the World-Honored Buddha and be sent back into human birth.

**REFRAIN:** Namu Amitabul.

If reborn as a man, may you be a prince of some kingdom;  
if reborn as a woman, may you be a princess.  
May you be born to parents with abundance, among many siblings;  
may you live long with your spouse and be blessed with many sons and daughters.

**REFRAIN:** Namu Amitabul.

**MUDANG:** Namu, namu Amitabul, Gwanseeum Bosal.

*After splitting the cloth into three paths, the Mudang removes her hat and enters between them holding a knife. She rubs and twists the cloth with the knife, releases it, dances while winding and unwinding the cloth, then gathers it and brings it to Yeonggam. Yeonggam passes it back. The Mudang returns to the center, performs a final dance, lays down the cloth, and makes a full bow.*

*When the* gut *is completely finished, all characters return, bow to the audience, perform the final ensemble dance, and exit.*

## After the Performance

The source records that the masks used in the performance were traditionally burned after the mask dance ended. This act was understood as sending away the **aek-un (액운)**—misfortune or harmful fortune—of both the performers and the spectators.

## Translation Note: “Awang Imgeum-ui Manseya”

The opening ritual phrase **아왕 임금의 만세야** has deliberately **not** been normalized to “Long live our King” or “Long live our Lord” in the working script.

Here the phrase occurs inside a shamanic ritual sequence rather than ordinary court or political speech. The surrounding invocations move among the human world, the Joseon state, local tutelary divinities, messengers of the dead, Buddhist Pure Land imagery, and underworld judges. Translating *imgeum* mechanically as *King* or *Lord* would therefore prejudge the ritual referent.

Until the expression is checked against comparable Hwanghae-do shamanic songs and the supporting scholarship, **Awang Imgeum-ui Manseya** is retained in romanization, with its ritual function preserved by context.
